# Guideless supplier master agreement

> **Draft for attorney review. Not legal advice. Do not send to a supplier.**
>
> Guideless has not yet determined whether it operates as a travel agent, a tour operator or a
> package organiser. Clauses marked **[POSITION-DEPENDENT]** are written on the assumption that
> Guideless is the organiser and is answerable to the traveler for the whole trip. If the attorney
> concludes Guideless acts as an agent for a disclosed principal, clauses 3, 11 and 12 need
> rewriting. See `README.md` in this directory.

## 1. What this agreement is

1.1 This is the master agreement. It sets the terms that apply to everything the Supplier provides
to Guideless.

1.2 One or more schedules are attached and form part of this agreement. Each schedule covers a type
of service: accommodation, experiences, transfers, event tickets. Where a schedule says something
different from this master agreement, the schedule wins for that service.

1.3 Individual bookings are made under this agreement. A booking does not create a separate
contract on different terms, whatever a confirmation email or booking portal says. See clause 22.

## 2. Term

2.1 This agreement starts on the effective date and continues until either party ends it under
clause 19.

2.2 Ending this agreement does not cancel bookings already confirmed. Those are honoured on these
terms until they are completed or cancelled under clause 9.

## 3. What Guideless is buying, and on whose behalf **[POSITION-DEPENDENT]**

3.1 Guideless buys the services described in each booking **in its own name and for its own
account**, and resells them to travelers as part of a trip Guideless organises.

3.2 The Supplier's customer is Guideless, not the traveler. The Supplier looks to Guideless for
payment and Guideless looks to the Supplier for performance.

3.3 The traveler is not a party to this agreement. Nothing here gives the traveler a direct right
against the Supplier, and nothing here limits any right the traveler has against Guideless.

3.4 Guideless does not travel with its groups. There is no Guideless guide or representative on
site. The Supplier delivers its service directly to travelers and must be able to do so without a
Guideless person present. Where a service needs someone to receive, count or supervise travelers,
that is the Supplier's job unless the schedule says otherwise.

> _Note for the attorney: 3.1 to 3.3 assume the organiser position. Under an agency model the
> Supplier's customer would be the traveler, Guideless would contract as agent, and 3.2 would be
> reversed. 3.4 is a product fact, not a legal position, and stays either way._

## 4. Rates and allocation

4.1 Rates are set out in the schedule or in a written rate sheet the parties both sign. Rates are
**net to Guideless**, exclusive of any commission, unless the schedule says otherwise.

4.2 Rates are fixed for the period stated. The Supplier may not increase a rate for a booking it has
already confirmed.

4.3 Where the Supplier holds an allocation of rooms, seats or places for Guideless, the schedule
states how many and until when. Guideless may release any part of an allocation before the release
date at no cost.

4.4 Rates are confidential. See clause 16.

4.5 Rates are quoted in `[CURRENCY]`. Guideless sells to travelers in US dollars and accepts the
exchange risk between the two. The Supplier may not adjust a confirmed rate because of a currency
movement.

## 5. Taxes and charges

5.1 Rates include all taxes, service charges and fees the Supplier is required to collect, except
those listed in the schedule as payable locally by the traveler.

5.2 Anything payable locally by the traveler must be listed in the schedule with its amount or rate.
Guideless publishes these to travelers before they book. A charge that is not in the schedule may
not be collected from a traveler.

> _Note: 5.2 exists because consumer travel rules in most of the jurisdictions in play require the
> total price and any unavoidable local charges to be disclosed before purchase._

## 6. How bookings are made

6.1 Guideless sends booking requests by email to the address in clause 21, or through the Supplier's
booking system where one is agreed in writing.

6.2 A booking request states the service, the date, the number of travelers, the names where the
service needs them, and any special requirements.

6.3 **The Supplier must confirm or decline in writing within `[CONFIRMATION HOURS, e.g. 48]`
hours.** A confirmation must include a confirmation reference. Silence is not acceptance.

6.4 Guideless records the Supplier's confirmation reference and the cancellation deadline against
the booking in its own systems. A Supplier who changes either must tell Guideless in writing.

6.5 Guideless may add travelers or services to an existing booking at any time up to the date of
service, including while the group is travelling, subject to availability. The Supplier will treat
a late addition as a new booking at the agreed rate and confirm it under 6.3.

> _Note: 6.5 reflects the product. Travelers can buy an extra night, a dinner or an activity during
> the trip, so the Supplier must expect same-day requests rather than a single cut-off._

## 7. Names and traveler details

7.1 Guideless provides traveler names and any details the service genuinely needs, and no more.

7.2 The Supplier may not require personal data it does not need to deliver the service. Requests for
passport numbers, dates of birth or dietary information must be justified by the service or by law.

7.3 Name changes are handled in the schedule. Where a schedule is silent, Guideless may change a
name up to `[NAME CHANGE DAYS]` days before the service at no charge.

## 8. Payment

8.1 Guideless pays `[PAYMENT TERMS, e.g. 30 days from invoice]` unless the schedule sets a deposit
and balance schedule, in which case the schedule governs.

8.2 Invoices must show the confirmation reference, the dates, the travelers or quantity, the rate
and the total. An invoice without a confirmation reference may be returned.

8.3 Guideless pays by `[PAYMENT METHOD]` to the account verified under the onboarding checklist.
**The Supplier may not change bank details by email alone.** A change must be confirmed by a call to
a number already on file. Guideless is not liable for payment to an account it was told to use by a
fraudulent instruction that followed this rule.

8.4 The Supplier may charge interest on genuinely late payment at `[LATE INTEREST RATE]`. It may not
cancel a confirmed booking for late payment without first giving Guideless `[CURE DAYS]` days'
written notice and a chance to pay.

8.5 Guideless may set off money the Supplier owes it against money it owes the Supplier.

> _Note: 8.3 is a fraud control, not boilerplate. Supplier-payment redirection fraud is common in
> travel and the money is rarely recoverable._

## 9. Cancellation and changes by Guideless

9.1 Guideless may cancel or reduce a booking free of charge up to the cancellation deadline in the
schedule.

9.2 After that deadline the charges in the schedule apply. Charges must be a genuine estimate of the
Supplier's loss, not a penalty, and the Supplier must reduce them by anything it recovers from
reselling.

9.3 Guideless will tell the Supplier as soon as it knows that a departure will not run.

9.4 Where a trip does not reach its minimum number of travelers, Guideless may cancel the whole
booking on the terms in the schedule. Guideless's minimum for a departure is published before
travelers book and is currently `[MINIMUM PER DEPARTURE]`.

## 10. Cancellation and changes by the Supplier

10.1 The Supplier may not cancel a confirmed booking except for force majeure under clause 12 or
non-payment under clause 8.4.

10.2 If the Supplier cannot deliver what it confirmed, it must **provide a service of an equivalent
or higher standard at its own cost**, and tell Guideless in writing before the traveler is affected
wherever that is possible.

10.3 If it cannot do that, it must refund everything paid for the affected service within
`[REFUND DAYS]` days, and pay Guideless's reasonable direct costs of putting the traveler somewhere
else. Those costs are capped at clause 11.

10.4 **Overbooking is not force majeure.** A Supplier who has oversold must move the traveler at its
own cost to a property or service of an equivalent or higher standard, in the same area, and pay any
resulting transport cost.

10.5 A change the Supplier makes that materially affects the traveler — a different property, a
different vessel, a different start time by more than `[MATERIAL CHANGE MINUTES]` minutes — must be
notified to Guideless immediately, whatever the hour.

## 11. Liability, indemnity and limits **[POSITION-DEPENDENT]**

11.1 Each party is responsible for its own negligence and for that of its staff and subcontractors.

11.2 The Supplier indemnifies Guideless against claims, losses and reasonable legal costs arising
from the Supplier's own act, omission, negligence, breach of this agreement, or failure to hold a
licence or insurance it warranted it held.

11.3 Guideless indemnifies the Supplier against claims arising from Guideless's own act or omission,
including incorrect information Guideless gave the Supplier.

11.4 Because Guideless sells the trip to the traveler, a traveler's claim about the Supplier's
service will usually be made against Guideless. The Supplier agrees that Guideless may recover from
it, under 11.2, what Guideless properly pays a traveler for the Supplier's failure.

11.5 Neither party limits liability for death or personal injury caused by its negligence, for
fraud, or for anything else that cannot be limited by law.

11.6 Subject to 11.5, each party's total liability under this agreement in any twelve-month period
is limited to `[LIABILITY CAP — e.g. the greater of the sums paid under this agreement in that
period, or a fixed amount]`.

11.7 Neither party is liable to the other for loss of profit, loss of business or indirect loss,
except that money Guideless properly pays a traveler under 11.4 is a direct loss.

> _Note: 11.4 and 11.7 are the commercial heart of this clause under the organiser position. They
> are what makes the Supplier's failure recoverable rather than absorbed. Under an agency model they
> would come out._

## 12. Force majeure

12.1 Neither party is in breach if it is prevented from performing by something genuinely outside
its control: natural disaster, war, terrorism, civil unrest, epidemic, government action, or the
closure of airspace, borders or the venue.

12.2 A strike or staff shortage at the Supplier is not force majeure. Nor is overbooking, financial
difficulty, or the withdrawal of the Supplier's own licence.

12.3 The party affected must tell the other as soon as it can and do what it reasonably can to
reduce the effect.

12.4 **Money already paid for a service that force majeure prevents is refunded to Guideless within
`[FORCE MAJEURE REFUND DAYS]` days**, less any cost the Supplier has genuinely and unavoidably
already incurred and can evidence. The Supplier may offer a credit but may not require Guideless to
take one.

12.5 If force majeure continues for more than `[FORCE MAJEURE TERMINATION DAYS]` days, either party
may cancel the affected booking with no further liability beyond the refund in 12.4.

> _Note: 12.4 matters more than it looks. Guideless has to refund its travelers under its published
> cancellation policy and, if the Package Travel Directive applies, on a fixed statutory deadline. A
> supplier credit that Guideless cannot pass on leaves Guideless funding the refund itself._

## 13. Standards, licences and permissions

13.1 The Supplier warrants that it holds every licence, registration, permit and classification its
business requires, and will keep them for the term. It will tell Guideless within
`[NOTIFICATION DAYS]` days if any is suspended, refused or withdrawn.

13.2 The Supplier warrants that it complies with the law where it operates, including health and
safety, employment, accessibility, food hygiene where relevant, and rules specific to its activity.

13.3 The Supplier warrants that it does not use forced labour or child labour, and that it pays its
staff at least the legal minimum.

13.4 The Supplier will provide copies of its licences and its insurance certificate on request, and
at least once a year.

13.5 The service must match what the Supplier described and what Guideless has published to
travelers on the basis of that description. If the Supplier changes something material — a
renovation, a closed pool, a changed menu, a moved meeting point — it must tell Guideless in writing
before travelers are affected.

## 14. Health, safety and incidents

14.1 The Supplier is responsible for the safety of travelers while they are in its care.

14.2 The Supplier will carry out and keep current a written risk assessment for any activity with a
material risk, and will follow its own safety procedures.

14.3 **Incident reporting.** The Supplier must contact Guideless **immediately, at any hour**, if a
traveler is injured, hospitalised, arrested, missing, or involved in anything likely to become a
complaint or a claim. It must also report a near miss that could have caused serious injury.

14.4 The Guideless emergency contact is `[24/7 PHONE NUMBER]`, with `[EMERGENCY EMAIL]` as backup.
The Supplier's emergency contact is `[SUPPLIER 24/7 PHONE]` and `[SUPPLIER EMERGENCY CONTACT NAME]`.
Both parties will keep these current. A voicemail box is not an emergency contact.

14.5 The Supplier will preserve records, photographs, CCTV and witness details relating to an
incident for at least `[RECORD RETENTION YEARS]` years, and will co-operate with Guideless and its
insurers.

14.6 The Supplier will not ask an injured traveler to sign a waiver, settlement or statement of
fault without telling Guideless first.

> _Note: 14.3 and 14.4 exist because no Guideless staff member is on site. The Supplier is the only
> party present when something goes wrong, so the reporting duty has to be explicit and immediate._

## 15. Insurance

15.1 The Supplier will keep, for the term and for `[TAIL YEARS]` years afterwards, at least:

| Cover                                     | Minimum limit                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Public / commercial general liability     | `[LIMIT]` per occurrence       |
| Professional indemnity where relevant     | `[LIMIT]`                      |
| Employer's liability where it has staff   | `[LIMIT]` or the legal minimum |
| Motor / passenger liability for transport | `[LIMIT]` or the legal minimum |
| Marine or activity-specific cover         | `[LIMIT]` — see the schedule   |

15.2 The Supplier will name Guideless as an additional insured where the activity warrants it, and
will provide a certificate of insurance naming Guideless before the first booking and on each
renewal.

15.3 The Supplier will tell Guideless if cover is cancelled, not renewed, or reduced below these
limits.

> _Note: the limits are deliberately blank. They should be set by the insurance broker against the
> activity — a boat operator and a hotel do not warrant the same numbers._

## 16. Confidentiality

16.1 Neither party will disclose the other's confidential information, including rates, allocation,
traveler numbers and commercial terms, except to staff and advisers who need it.

16.2 The Supplier will not disclose that it works with Guideless, or use the Guideless name or
logo, without written permission. Guideless may name the Supplier to travelers, and to its insurers
and advisers, as the provider of the service.

16.3 This clause survives the end of the agreement by `[CONFIDENTIALITY YEARS]` years.

## 17. Personal data

17.1 Guideless passes the Supplier only the traveler data the service needs.

17.2 Travelers are in France and Monaco while the services are delivered and the Supplier is
established in Europe, so the GDPR applies to this processing. Each party will comply with it.

17.3 **The parties will record, in the schedule, whether the Supplier acts as a processor on
Guideless's instructions or as a controller in its own right.** A hotel that keeps a guest record
for its own legal and commercial purposes is usually a controller for that record. A supplier who
only receives a name list to run one activity is usually a processor.

17.4 Where the Supplier is a processor it will: act only on Guideless's written instructions; keep
the data confidential; apply appropriate security; not engage a sub-processor without permission;
help Guideless answer a traveler's data request; and delete or return the data when the service is
finished, except where law requires it to keep it. A separate data processing agreement will be
attached as `[DPA REFERENCE]`.

17.5 Either party will tell the other **within 24 hours** of becoming aware of a personal data
breach affecting the other's travelers or data.

17.6 Neither party will use traveler data for its own marketing without the traveler's own consent.
The Supplier may not add Guideless travelers to a mailing list.

> _Note: 17.3 is left open on purpose. Getting controller-versus-processor wrong is the most common
> mistake in supplier data terms, and the answer differs between the hotel and the boat operator._

## 18. Subcontracting

18.1 The Supplier may not subcontract a service without Guideless's written permission, except where
the schedule allows it.

18.2 Where subcontracting is allowed, the Supplier stays fully responsible for the service and for
the subcontractor's acts, and must ensure the subcontractor meets clauses 13, 14, 15 and 17.

## 19. Ending this agreement

19.1 Either party may end this agreement on `[NOTICE MONTHS]` months' written notice.

19.2 Either party may end it immediately if the other: commits a material breach and does not fix it
within `[CURE DAYS]` days of being asked; becomes insolvent or enters an insolvency process; or
loses a licence it needs.

19.3 Guideless may end it immediately, and cancel confirmed bookings without charge, if the Supplier
breaches clause 13.3, or if Guideless reasonably concludes on evidence that continuing would put
travelers at risk.

19.4 Clauses 11, 14.5, 16, 17 and 20 survive the end of this agreement.

## 20. Anti-bribery and sanctions

20.1 Each party will comply with applicable anti-bribery and anti-corruption law, including the US
Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, and will not offer or accept an improper payment.

20.2 Each party warrants that it is not subject to sanctions that would make this agreement unlawful.

## 21. Notices

21.1 Notices go by email, and are effective when sent unless the sender is told delivery failed.

21.2 To Guideless: `[GUIDELESS NOTICE EMAIL]`, copied to `partners@guidelesstravel.com`.
To the Supplier: `[SUPPLIER NOTICE EMAIL]`.

21.3 Emergency contact under clause 14.4 is by telephone first, then email. An emergency notice is
not validly given by email alone.

## 22. The Supplier's own terms do not apply

22.1 This agreement and its schedules are the whole agreement about what they cover.

22.2 **The Supplier's own standard terms do not apply**, whether they arrive on a confirmation, an
invoice, a booking portal, a click-through or the back of a voucher, and whether or not a Guideless
employee clicked or signed to complete a booking. Nothing a booking system requires anyone to accept
in order to make a booking varies this agreement.

22.3 A change to this agreement is only effective if it is in writing and signed by both parties.

> _Note: 22.2 is drafted broadly on purpose. Most supplier terms arrive after the deal, inside a
> booking flow, and a narrower clause would not catch them._

## 23. General

23.1 Neither party may assign this agreement without the other's written consent, except that
Guideless may assign it to a company in the same group or to a buyer of its business.

23.2 Nothing here creates a partnership, joint venture or employment relationship.

23.3 If a clause is unenforceable, the rest stays in force.

23.4 A party's failure to enforce a term is not a waiver of it.

23.5 Nobody who is not a party may enforce this agreement, subject to clause 3.3.

## 24. Governing law and disputes

24.1 This agreement is governed by the law of `[GOVERNING LAW]`.

24.2 The parties will try in good faith to settle a dispute by discussion between senior people
before starting proceedings.

24.3 Disputes are subject to `[COURTS OR ARBITRATION FORUM]`.
